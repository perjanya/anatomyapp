Lymphatic System

Key points to remember:
Lymphatic vessels return excess interstitial fluid and escaped proteins to the bloodstream, helping maintain tissue fluid balance.
Valves support one-way lymph flow, assisted by collecting-vessel contraction and surrounding muscle activity.
Bone marrow and thymus are primary lymphoid organs; lymph nodes, spleen, and other lymphoid tissues support immune responses.
Lymphatic obstruction can cause lymphoedema, while lymphatic pathways also provide routes for tumour spread.

Structure of lymph capillaries

Lymph capillaries are thin-walled vessels carrying lymph

They are lined by endothelial cells

Thin layer of smooth muscles (maintain tone) and collagen fibres surround these vessels

Located in all organs

  • Lymph capillaries coalesce into larger lymphatic collectors
  • while conventional initial lymphatics are absent from parenchymal brain tissue, drainage occurs via the glymphatic system (astrocyte AQP4 water channels) into newly described dural meningeal lymphatic vessels that exit along cranial nerves and dural sinuses to deep cervical nodes.

Lymph formation

  • Protein extravasation from the microcirculation into the interstitial fluid
  • Parenchymal organs may also contribute minimally to the protein component of the lymph

Lymph circulation

  • Directional lymph flow - is controlled by unidirectional valves in lymph collectors
  • Segment of lymph collectors between adjacent valves - called Lymphangion
  • More distal lymphangion contracts in synchrony with valves - facilitate unidirectional flow

Lymph proteome changes with disease status

Components of lymphatic system

  • Lymph - colorless, watery, bodily fluid carried by the lymphatic system, consisting mainly of white blood cells
  • Lymphatic vessels - Lymph capillaries and lymph collectors
  • Lymphatic organs

Components of lymphatic system

Primary lymphoid organs

  • Thymus
  • Bone marrow

Secondary lymphoid organs

  • Simple
    • Diffuse lymphatic tissue (Mucosa associated lymphatic tissue MALT)
    • Solitary lymph nodes
    • Lymph node aggregates
  • Intermediate
    • Tonsil
  • Organs
    • Lymph nodes
    • Spleen

Functions

Primary lymphoid organs

  • Lymphocyte education centers
  • T cells - in the thymus
  • B cells - in the bone marrow

Secondary lymphoid organs

  • For removal of foreign bodies and dead cells

Clinical importance - lymphedema

Swelling in the part of the body due to accumulation of lymph

Commonly seen after removal of axillary lymph nodes and lymphatics as part of axillary dissection during breast cancer treatment

Clinical importance - spread of tumors

Cancers can spread via lymphatics (lymphatic metastasis) 

Eg: Breast cancer
