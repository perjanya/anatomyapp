Glossary

Simple meanings of the main terms used in this book. Terms are grouped by topic for easy revision.

1. General terms, position and direction

Term

Simple meaning

Anatomy

Study of the structure of the body.

Physiology

Study of how the body works.

Gross anatomy

Study of body structures visible without a microscope.

Histology

Study of tissues using a microscope.

Embryology

Study of development before birth.

Dissection

Carefully separating tissues to study their structure.

Cell

The smallest living unit of the body.

Tissue

A group of cells and surrounding material working together.

Organ

A body part made of different tissues working together.

Organ system

A group of organs performing related functions.

Anatomical position

Standing upright, facing forward, arms at the sides and palms facing forward.

Supine

Lying face up.

Prone

Lying face down.

Anterior

Towards the front of the body.

Posterior

Towards the back of the body.

Superior

Towards the head or above another structure.

Inferior

Towards the feet or below another structure.

Medial

Nearer the body’s midline.

Lateral

Farther from the body’s midline.

Median

Exactly in the midline.

Proximal

Nearer the attachment or beginning of a structure.

Distal

Farther from the attachment or beginning of a structure.

Superficial

Nearer the body surface.

Deep

Farther from the body surface.

Internal

Inside a structure.

External

Outside a structure.

Ipsilateral

On the same side of the body.

Contralateral

On opposite sides of the body.

Bilateral

Present on both sides.

Unilateral

Present on one side only.

Palmar

Relating to the palm of the hand.

Plantar

Relating to the sole of the foot.

Dorsum

The back of the hand or the upper surface of the foot.

2. Planes and movements

Term

Simple meaning

Plane

An imaginary flat surface passing through the body.

Section

A view obtained by cutting or imaging through a structure.

Median plane

A vertical plane dividing the body into equal right and left halves.

Sagittal plane

A vertical plane dividing the body into right and left parts.

Parasagittal plane

A sagittal plane lying beside the median plane.

Coronal or frontal plane

A vertical plane dividing the body into front and back parts.

Transverse or axial plane

A plane dividing the body into upper and lower parts.

Oblique plane

A plane passing through the body at a slant.

Longitudinal section

A section along the length of a structure.

Cross-section

A section across the length of a structure.

Axis of movement

An imaginary line around which movement occurs.

Flexion

Bending a joint, usually decreasing the angle between body parts.

Extension

Straightening a joint, usually increasing the angle between body parts.

Abduction

Movement away from the midline; fingers and toes use their own reference lines.

Adduction

Movement towards the midline; fingers and toes use their own reference lines.

Rotation

Turning a body part around its long axis.

Medial rotation

Turning the front of a limb towards the body’s midline.

Lateral rotation

Turning the front of a limb away from the body’s midline.

Circumduction

A circular movement in which the moving part traces a cone.

Pronation

Rotating the forearm so the palm faces backwards or downwards.

Supination

Rotating the forearm so the palm faces forwards or upwards.

Dorsiflexion

Moving the top of the foot towards the shin.

Plantarflexion

Pointing the foot and toes downwards.

Inversion

Turning the sole of the foot inwards.

Eversion

Turning the sole of the foot outwards.

Opposition

Moving the thumb across the palm to touch a fingertip.

Reposition

Returning the thumb from opposition to its resting position.

Elevation

Moving a body part upwards.

Depression

Moving a body part downwards.

Protraction

Moving a body part forwards, such as the jaw.

Retraction

Moving a body part backwards.

3. Basic tissues

Term

Simple meaning

Epithelium

Tissue that covers surfaces, lines spaces and forms many glands.

Simple epithelium

Epithelium with one layer of cells.

Stratified epithelium

Epithelium with two or more layers of cells.

Squamous

Having flat, thin cells.

Cuboidal

Having cells that are roughly cube-shaped.

Columnar

Having cells that are taller than they are wide.

Pseudostratified epithelium

One cell layer that looks like several layers.

Transitional epithelium

A specialised lining that stretches, as in the urinary bladder.

Apical surface

The cell surface facing an open space or the outside.

Basal surface

The bottom surface of an epithelial cell, facing its supporting layer.

Basement membrane

A thin supporting layer beneath epithelium.

Lumen

The space inside a tube or hollow organ.

Gland

A cell or group of cells that makes and releases a substance.

Secretion

The release of a substance made by a cell or gland.

Connective tissue

Tissue that supports, binds, protects or connects body structures.

Extracellular matrix

Material outside cells that surrounds and supports them.

Ground substance

The gel-like material between cells and fibres.

Fibroblast

A cell that makes fibres and other material in connective tissue.

Collagen

A strong protein that helps tissues resist pulling forces.

Elastic fibres

Fibres that allow tissues to stretch and return to shape.

Loose connective tissue

Connective tissue with loosely arranged fibres and plenty of space between them.

Dense connective tissue

Connective tissue containing many closely packed fibres.

Adipose tissue

Fat-storing tissue that also cushions and insulates the body.

Mesenchyme

Early developing tissue that gives rise to many supporting tissues.

Vascular

Containing blood vessels.

Avascular

Having no blood vessels.

Diffusion

Movement of particles from a region of higher to lower concentration.

Regeneration

Replacement of damaged cells with the same type of cells.

Fibrosis

Formation of excess fibrous tissue, often after injury.

Scar

Fibrous tissue that replaces damaged tissue during healing.

4. Bones and bone growth

Term

Simple meaning

Skeleton

The framework of bones and cartilage supporting the body.

Axial skeleton

Bones of the skull, vertebral column and chest.

Appendicular skeleton

Bones of the limbs and the girdles attaching them to the trunk.

Long bone

A bone longer than it is wide, with a shaft and two ends.

Short bone

A bone with roughly similar length, width and thickness.

Flat bone

A thin, flattened bone, such as the scapula.

Irregular bone

A bone with a complex shape, such as a vertebra.

Pneumatic bone

A bone containing an air-filled space.

Sesamoid bone

A bone that develops within a tendon, such as the patella.

Accessory bone

An extra bone separate from the usual bones of the skeleton.

Compact or cortical bone

Dense bone forming the strong outer layer of a bone.

Cancellous or spongy bone

Bone formed of a network of thin plates and bars with spaces between them.

Trabecula

A small plate or bar forming the framework of spongy bone.

Diaphysis

The shaft of a long bone.

Epiphysis

A part of a growing bone that develops from a secondary ossification centre.

Metaphysis

The region of a long bone between the shaft and the growth plate.

Epiphyseal or growth plate

Cartilage where a growing bone increases in length.

Epiphyseal line

The bony mark left after a growth plate closes.

Pressure epiphysis

An epiphysis that forms part of a joint and transmits force.

Traction epiphysis

An epiphysis at a muscle attachment that receives pulling force.

Atavistic epiphysis

An epiphysis corresponding to a separate bone in ancestral animals.

Aberrant epiphysis

An occasional epiphysis at a site where one is not usually present.

Periosteum

The connective tissue covering the outside of bone, except its joint surfaces.

Endosteum

The thin tissue lining internal bone surfaces.

Medullary cavity

The marrow-containing space inside the shaft of a long bone.

Bone marrow

Soft tissue within bones; red marrow produces blood cells.

Nutrient artery

A major artery entering a bone to supply its internal tissues.

Nutrient foramen

The opening through which a nutrient vessel enters a bone.

Ossification

Formation of bone.

Ossification centre

A site where bone formation begins.

Primary ossification centre

The first main site of bone formation, usually in the shaft.

Secondary ossification centre

A later site of bone formation, usually in an epiphysis.

Endochondral ossification

Bone formation by replacing an existing cartilage model.

Intramembranous ossification

Bone formation directly within connective tissue, without a cartilage model.

Bone collar

A thin sleeve of new bone around the shaft of a developing cartilage model.

Periosteal bud

Blood vessels and accompanying cells entering a developing bone.

Osteoprogenitor cell

A cell that can develop into an osteoblast.

Osteoblast

A cell that forms new bone matrix.

Osteocyte

A mature bone cell enclosed within bone matrix.

Osteoclast

A cell that breaks down and removes bone.

Osteoid

Newly formed bone matrix before minerals are deposited in it.

Calcification

Deposition of calcium salts in a tissue.

Mineralisation

Deposition of minerals that make a tissue harder.

Hypertrophy

Increase in the size of cells.

Proliferation

Increase in cell number through cell division.

Resorption

Breakdown and removal of tissue.

Bone remodelling

Renewal and reshaping of bone by removal and replacement.

Appositional growth

Growth by adding new tissue to a surface.

Interstitial growth

Growth from within a tissue.

Lamella

A thin layer of bone matrix.

Osteon

A cylindrical unit of compact bone arranged around a central canal.

Haversian canal

The central canal of an osteon, containing vessels and nerves.

Volkmann’s canal

A channel connecting blood vessels across compact bone.

Lacuna

A small space containing a cell in bone or cartilage.

Canaliculi

Tiny channels connecting bone cells with each other and nearby blood supply.

Enthesis

The site where a tendon or ligament attaches to bone.

5. Cartilage

Term

Simple meaning

Cartilage

Firm, flexible supporting tissue with cells in a gel-like matrix.

Chondroblast

A cell that produces cartilage matrix.

Chondrocyte

A mature cartilage cell lying in a lacuna.

Perichondrium

The connective tissue covering most cartilage.

Hyaline cartilage

Smooth, glassy-looking cartilage found at joints and in growth plates.

Elastic cartilage

Flexible cartilage containing elastic fibres, as in the external ear.

Fibrocartilage

Tough cartilage containing thick collagen bundles, as in intervertebral discs.

Articular cartilage

Smooth cartilage covering the bone surfaces of a synovial joint.

Calcified cartilage

Cartilage with mineral deposits in its matrix; it is not the same as bone.

Reserve zone

The growth-plate region containing relatively inactive cartilage cells.

Proliferative zone

The growth-plate region where cartilage cells divide and form columns.

Hypertrophic zone

The growth-plate region where cartilage cells enlarge.

Zone of calcification

The region where the cartilage matrix becomes mineralised.

Zone of ossification

The region where new bone is deposited on remaining calcified cartilage.

Primary spongiosa

Newly formed trabeculae containing calcified cartilage covered by bone.

Secondary spongiosa

More mature spongy bone formed by remodelling early trabeculae.

6. Fascia and skin

Term

Simple meaning

Fascia

Sheets of connective tissue that surround, support and connect structures.

Superficial fascia

Loose connective tissue beneath the skin, often containing fat.

Deep fascia

Tough connective tissue surrounding muscles and other deeper structures.

Intermuscular septum

A connective tissue partition separating groups of muscles.

Muscle compartment

A group of muscles, vessels and nerves enclosed by fascia and bone.

Retinaculum

A thickened fascial band holding tendons close to underlying structures.

Aponeurosis

A broad, flat tendon.

Raphe

A line where tissues from opposite sides join.

Interosseous membrane

A fibrous sheet connecting two bones.

Epidermis

The outer, avascular layer of the skin.

Dermis

The supporting skin layer containing vessels, nerves and glands.

Hypodermis

The tissue beneath the skin, often rich in fat.

Keratin

A tough protein found in the epidermis, hair and nails.

Melanin

The pigment that contributes to skin colour and protection from sunlight.

Hair follicle

The skin structure from which a hair grows.

Sebaceous gland

A gland that releases oily sebum into a hair follicle or onto skin.

Sweat gland

A gland that produces sweat.

Arrector pili

A tiny smooth muscle that raises a hair, producing goosebumps.

Dermatome

An area of skin supplied mainly by one spinal nerve root.

Dermatoglyphics

The study of skin ridge patterns on fingers, palms, toes and soles.

Friction ridges

Raised skin lines that form fingerprints and improve grip.

Skin tension lines

Lines showing the main direction of tension within the skin.

Incision

A cut made during a surgical procedure.

7. Joints

Term

Simple meaning

Joint or articulation

A place where two or more bones meet.

Fibrous joint

A joint where bones are joined by fibrous connective tissue.

Suture

A fibrous joint between skull bones.

Syndesmosis

A fibrous joint joined by a ligament or an interosseous membrane.

Gomphosis

The joint between a tooth and its socket.

Cartilaginous joint

A joint where bones are joined by cartilage.

Synchondrosis

A primary cartilaginous joint joined by hyaline cartilage.

Symphysis

A secondary cartilaginous joint with fibrocartilage between the bones.

Synovial joint

A joint with a fluid-containing cavity between its articulating surfaces.

Joint cavity

The space between the surfaces of a synovial joint.

Joint capsule

The tissue sleeve enclosing a synovial joint.

Fibrous capsule

The tough outer layer of a synovial joint capsule.

Synovial membrane

The inner lining that helps produce synovial fluid.

Synovial fluid

Fluid that lubricates a joint and nourishes its articular cartilage.

Ligament

A strong connective tissue band usually joining one bone to another.

Articular disc

A plate of fibrocartilage between joint surfaces.

Meniscus

A crescent-shaped pad of fibrocartilage within a joint.

Labrum

A fibrocartilage rim that deepens a joint socket.

Bursa

A small fluid-containing sac that reduces friction between moving structures.

Tendon sheath

A sleeve containing lubricating fluid around a tendon.

Plane joint

A synovial joint allowing mainly gliding movements.

Hinge joint

A synovial joint allowing mainly bending and straightening.

Pivot joint

A synovial joint allowing rotation around one axis.

Condyloid joint

A synovial joint with an oval surface fitting into an oval hollow.

Saddle joint

A joint whose surfaces are each curved inwards in one direction and outwards in another.

Ball-and-socket joint

A joint with a rounded head fitting into a socket, allowing movement in many directions.

Uniaxial

Allowing movement around one axis.

Biaxial

Allowing movement around two axes.

Multiaxial

Allowing movement around several axes.

8. Blood vessels and circulation

Term

Simple meaning

Artery

A blood vessel carrying blood away from the heart.

Vein

A blood vessel carrying blood towards the heart.

Arteriole

A small artery that controls blood flow into capillaries.

Capillary

A tiny vessel where substances pass between blood and tissues.

Venule

A small vessel collecting blood from capillaries.

Endothelium

The thin cell lining inside blood and lymphatic vessels.

Tunica intima

The innermost layer of a blood vessel wall.

Tunica media

The middle vessel layer, containing mainly smooth muscle and elastic tissue.

Tunica adventitia

The outer supporting connective tissue layer of a blood vessel.

Vasa vasorum

Small vessels supplying the walls of large blood vessels.

Venous valve

A flap inside a vein that helps prevent backward blood flow.

Anastomosis

A connection between vessels or other tubular structures.

Collateral circulation

Blood flow through an alternative route around a blockage.

Anatomical end artery

An artery with no connections providing an alternative arterial supply.

Functional end artery

An artery whose connections cannot provide enough blood if it is blocked.

Arteriovenous anastomosis

A direct connection between an artery and a vein, bypassing capillaries.

Portal system

A circulation in which blood passes through two capillary beds before returning to the heart.

Perfusion

Delivery of blood through a tissue.

Ischaemia

Inadequate blood supply to a tissue.

Infarction

Death of tissue caused by loss of its blood supply.

Thrombus

A blood clot formed inside a vessel or the heart.

Thrombosis

Formation of a thrombus.

Embolus

Material carried in blood that can lodge in and block a vessel.

Embolism

Blockage of a vessel by travelling material, such as a blood clot.

Haemorrhage

Bleeding from a damaged blood vessel.

Haematoma

A collection of blood outside blood vessels.

9. Muscles

Term

Simple meaning

Muscle fibre

A muscle cell.

Fascicle

A bundle of muscle fibres; in a nerve, a bundle of nerve fibres.

Skeletal muscle

Striated muscle that usually moves bones and is mainly under voluntary control.

Cardiac muscle

The muscle of the heart wall that pumps blood.

Smooth muscle

Non-striated muscle in structures such as the intestine and blood vessels.

Striated

Showing alternating light and dark bands under a microscope.

Voluntary

Usually under conscious control.

Involuntary

Working without conscious control.

Muscle belly

The fleshy, contractile part of a muscle.

Tendon

A strong cord or band connecting a muscle to its attachment.

Origin

The attachment that usually moves less during a muscle’s action.

Insertion

The attachment that usually moves more during a muscle’s action.

Muscle architecture

The arrangement of muscle fibres in relation to their tendons.

Parallel muscle

A muscle whose fibres run mainly along its length.

Fusiform muscle

A spindle-shaped muscle with a thick middle and narrow ends.

Pennate muscle

A muscle whose fibres attach obliquely to a tendon, like a feather.

Unipennate

Having fibres attached to one side of a tendon.

Bipennate

Having fibres attached to both sides of a tendon.

Multipennate

Having several feather-like groups of fibres.

Agonist or prime mover

A muscle mainly responsible for a particular movement.

Antagonist

A muscle whose action opposes a particular movement.

Synergist

A muscle that helps another muscle perform a movement.

Fixator

A muscle that holds a body part steady while another part moves.

Muscle tone

The slight, ongoing activity that helps muscles maintain posture.

Endomysium

Connective tissue around an individual muscle fibre.

Perimysium

Connective tissue around a muscle fascicle.

Epimysium

Connective tissue around a whole skeletal muscle.

Motor unit

One motor neuron and all the muscle fibres it supplies.

Neuromuscular junction

The site where a motor neuron communicates with a skeletal muscle fibre.

10. Nervous system

Term

Simple meaning

Central nervous system — CNS

The brain and spinal cord.

Peripheral nervous system — PNS

Nerves and ganglia outside the brain and spinal cord.

Neuron

A nerve cell that receives and sends signals.

Cell body

The part of a neuron containing its nucleus.

Dendrite

A branch that usually carries signals towards a neuron’s cell body.

Axon

A process that usually carries signals away from a neuron’s cell body.

Nerve fibre

An axon together with its surrounding coverings.

Nerve

A bundle of nerve fibres outside the brain and spinal cord.

Tract

A bundle of axons within the brain or spinal cord.

Ganglion

A collection of neuron cell bodies outside the brain and spinal cord.

Nucleus, in neuroanatomy

A group of neuron cell bodies within the brain or spinal cord.

Sensory or afferent fibre

A fibre carrying information towards the central nervous system.

Motor or efferent fibre

A fibre carrying commands from the central nervous system to an effector.

Mixed nerve

A nerve containing both sensory and motor fibres.

Receptor

A specialised cell or nerve ending that detects a change.

Effector

A muscle or gland that carries out a response.

Somatic nervous system

The system concerned with body sensation and control of skeletal muscles.

Autonomic nervous system

The system regulating internal organs, glands, smooth muscle and cardiac muscle.

Sympathetic division

The autonomic division that prepares the body for activity and stress.

Parasympathetic division

The autonomic division that supports functions such as digestion and recovery.

Synapse

A junction where a neuron communicates with another cell.

Neurotransmitter

A chemical messenger released by a neuron.

Reflex

An automatic response to a stimulus.

Reflex arc

The nerve pathway producing a reflex response.

Glial cells or neuroglia

Cells that support, protect and maintain neurons.

Myelin

An insulating covering around some axons that speeds signal transmission.

Schwann cell

A supporting cell that forms myelin around peripheral axons.

Oligodendrocyte

A cell that forms myelin in the brain and spinal cord.

Astrocyte

A glial cell that supports neurons and helps control their surroundings.

Microglia

Immune cells that remove damaged material in the brain and spinal cord.

Node of Ranvier

A small gap between neighbouring myelin segments along an axon.

Endoneurium

Delicate connective tissue around an individual peripheral nerve fibre.

Perineurium

The protective sheath around a nerve fascicle.

Epineurium

The outer connective tissue around a whole peripheral nerve.

Dorsal root

The sensory root of a spinal nerve.

Ventral root

The motor root of a spinal nerve.

Dorsal root ganglion

A collection of sensory neuron cell bodies on a dorsal root.

Ramus

A branch of a nerve or another structure.

Nerve plexus

A network formed by joining and dividing nerve branches.

Grey matter

Nervous tissue rich in neuron cell bodies, dendrites and synapses.

White matter

Nervous tissue containing mainly axons, many covered by myelin.

Cerebrum

The largest brain region, involved in thought, sensation and voluntary movement.

Cerebellum

The brain region that helps coordinate movement and balance.

Brainstem

The midbrain, pons and medulla, connecting higher brain regions with the spinal cord.

Thalamus

A brain region that relays much sensory information to the cerebral cortex.

Hypothalamus

A brain region regulating functions such as temperature, hunger and hormone control.

Meninges

The three protective coverings of the brain and spinal cord.

Cerebrospinal fluid — CSF

Fluid surrounding and cushioning the brain and spinal cord.

Neuroplasticity

The nervous system’s ability to change its connections and activity.

11. Lymphatic system

Term

Simple meaning

Interstitial fluid

Fluid in the spaces between tissue cells.

Lymph

Fluid that has entered lymphatic vessels from the tissues.

Lymphatic capillary

A tiny, blind-ended vessel that collects fluid from tissues.

Lymphatic vessel

A vessel carrying lymph towards the venous circulation.

Lymph node

A small organ that filters lymph and helps organise immune responses.

Afferent lymphatic vessel

A vessel carrying lymph into a lymph node.

Efferent lymphatic vessel

A vessel carrying lymph out of a lymph node.

Lymphatic drainage

Removal of tissue fluid through lymphatic vessels.

Thoracic duct

The main lymphatic duct, draining most of the body into veins on the left.

Right lymphatic duct

A duct draining the right upper part of the body into veins on the right.

Lacteal

A lymphatic capillary in the small intestine that absorbs dietary fats.

Lymphoid tissue

Tissue rich in immune cells that help defend the body.

Lymphocyte

A white blood cell involved in immune defence.

Oedema

Swelling caused by excess fluid between tissue cells.

Lymphoedema

Swelling caused by impaired lymphatic drainage.

Sentinel lymph node

One of the first lymph nodes receiving drainage from a particular area or tumour.

12. Clinical terms

Term

Simple meaning

Fracture

A break in a bone.

Callus

Repair tissue that forms around and between the ends of a healing fracture.

Growth-plate injury

Damage to the cartilage responsible for a growing bone’s increase in length.

Osteomyelitis

Infection involving bone and usually its marrow.

Avascular necrosis

Death of bone tissue because its blood supply is lost.

Osteoporosis

Reduced bone strength that increases the risk of fracture.

Sprain

An injury that stretches or tears a ligament.

Strain

An injury that stretches or tears a muscle or tendon.

Dislocation

Complete loss of normal contact between joint surfaces.

Subluxation

Partial loss of normal contact between joint surfaces.

Arthritis

Inflammation of a joint.

Osteoarthritis

A joint disease involving cartilage damage and changes in surrounding tissues.

Compartment syndrome

Harmful pressure within a closed tissue compartment that reduces blood supply.

Atrophy

A decrease in the size of cells, a tissue or an organ.

Paralysis

Loss of the ability to move a muscle or body part.

Paresis

Partial loss of muscle strength.

Paraesthesia

An unusual sensation, such as tingling or pins and needles.

Inflammation

The body’s response to injury or infection, often causing pain, warmth and swelling.

Necrosis

Death of cells or tissue within the living body.

Metastasis

Spread of cancer from its original site to another part of the body.

Stem cell

A cell that can renew itself and produce specialised cells.

Differentiation

The process by which a cell becomes specialised.

Cell reprogramming

Changing a cell’s identity or developmental state.

Tissue plasticity

The ability of cells or tissues to change their properties.

Immunohistochemistry — IHC

Using antibodies to identify particular proteins in tissue sections.

Cell marker

A feature, often a protein, used to help identify a cell type.

Epithelial–mesenchymal transition — EMT

A change in which epithelial cells acquire more mobile, less tightly attached features.

Artificial cornea

A manufactured optical device used to replace the eye’s clear front window.

Osteo-odonto-keratoprosthesis — OOKP

An artificial cornea supported by a piece of the patient’s tooth and surrounding bone.

Rehabilitation

Training and treatment to improve function after illness or injury.
