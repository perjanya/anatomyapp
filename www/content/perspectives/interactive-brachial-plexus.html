Interactive Brachial Plexus

Interactivity drives learning: revealing the brachial plexus one layer at a time

A fully labelled brachial plexus diagram is a useful reference. On a first pass, though, students can spend more time finding labels than following the fibres. Progressive reveal keeps the diagram in view and brings in one group of labels at a time: roots → trunks → divisions → cords → terminal branches. Learners can focus on the current level or keep earlier labels visible to trace connections across levels.

Interactivity drives learning when each action has a purpose. In this activity, learners first inspect the unlabelled diagram, predict what comes next, and then reveal the labels to check their reasoning. Short questions provide immediate feedback. A final challenge asks learners to locate each region without labels.

How to build an activity like this with generative AI

  1. Define the learning task. Decide what students should be able to do, such as tracing a route from a spinal nerve root to a terminal branch.
  2. Supply a verified diagram and a sequence. Give the AI tool an SVG you have checked, the label groups, and the relationships you want students to notice. Ask it to preserve the diagram and reveal labels in stages.
  3. Generate a browser prototype. Ask for a self-contained HTML file with reveal and reset controls, a question at each stage, and feedback that explains the answer.
  4. Review and test. Check every connection, label and answer. Try the activity on a phone and with a keyboard, then refine any step where the interaction distracts from the anatomy.
