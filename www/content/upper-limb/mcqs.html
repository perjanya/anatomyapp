MCQs – Upper Limb

Choose an answer before revealing the explanation.

Pectoral Region, Axilla & Breast

1. Which structure pierces the clavipectoral fascia?

  1. Medial pectoral nerve
  2. Cephalic vein
  3. Axillary artery
  4. Lateral cord of brachial plexus
Reveal answer

B — Cephalic vein (The clavipectoral fascia is pierced by the cephalic vein, thoracoacromial artery, lateral pectoral nerve, and lymphatic vessels draining to apical axillary nodes).

2. The suspensory ligaments of Cooper, which cause skin dimpling when infiltrated by carcinoma of the breast, connect the skin of the breast to:

  1. Pectoralis minor fascia
  2. Retromammary space
  3. Pectoralis major fascia
  4. Clavicular periosteum
Reveal answer

C — Pectoralis major fascia (The suspensory ligaments of Cooper run from the deep layer of superficial fascia/dermis to the deep fascia overlying pectoralis major).

3. Level II axillary lymph nodes in surgical oncology staging are located:

  1. Lateral to the lateral border of pectoralis minor
  2. Deep to pectoralis minor
  3. Medial to the medial border of pectoralis minor
  4. Along the subscapular vessels
Reveal answer

B — Deep to pectoralis minor (Surgical levels of axillary lymph nodes: Level I = lateral/inferior to pectoralis minor; Level II = deep/posterior to pectoralis minor; Level III = medial/superior to pectoralis minor).

4. Which muscle forms the primary muscular component of the anterior wall of the axilla?

  1. Pectoralis minor
  2. Latissimus dorsi
  3. Pectoralis major
  4. Subscapularis
Reveal answer

C — Pectoralis major (The anterior wall of the axilla is formed primarily by pectoralis major, assisted by pectoralis minor and subclavius).

5. The lateral thoracic artery typically arises from which part of the axillary artery?

  1. First part
  2. Second part
  3. Third part
  4. Thyrocervical trunk
Reveal answer

B — Second part (The second part of the axillary artery, located deep to pectoralis minor, gives off the thoracoacromial and lateral thoracic arteries).

Shoulder & Scapular Region

6. The quadrangular space transmits which combination of neurovascular structures?

  1. Suprascapular nerve and suprascapular artery
  2. Radial nerve and profunda brachii artery
  3. Axillary nerve and posterior circumflex humeral artery
  4. Lower subscapular nerve and circumflex scapular artery
Reveal answer

C — Axillary nerve and posterior circumflex humeral artery (The quadrangular space is bounded by subscapularis/teres minor superiorly, teres major inferiorly, long head of triceps medially, and surgical neck of humerus laterally).

7. In the arterial anastomosis around the body of the scapula, the circumflex scapular artery anastomoses with branches of which artery?

  1. Suprascapular artery
  2. Lateral thoracic artery
  3. Thoracoacromial artery
  4. Posterior circumflex humeral artery
Reveal answer

A — Suprascapular artery (Circumflex scapular from the subscapular artery anastomoses with the suprascapular artery and the deep branch of the transverse cervical/dorsal scapular artery).

8. Which rotator cuff muscle inserts into the lesser tubercle of the humerus?

  1. Supraspinatus
  2. Infraspinatus
  3. Teres minor
  4. Subscapularis
Reveal answer

D — Subscapularis (Subscapularis inserts into the lesser tubercle, whereas supraspinatus, infraspinatus, and teres minor insert into the superior, middle, and inferior facets of the greater tubercle).

9. The primary muscle responsible for the initiation of arm abduction (first 15°) is:

  1. Deltoid (multipennate acromial fibers)
  2. Supraspinatus
  3. Infraspinatus
  4. Serratus anterior
Reveal answer

B — Supraspinatus (Supraspinatus initiates abduction from 0°–15°; the deltoid takes over from 15°–90°, and serratus anterior/trapezius enable overhead rotation).

10. A patient presents with weakness in lateral (external) rotation of the shoulder following a posterior blow to the scapular region. Which nerve is most likely compressed at the spinoglenoid (greater scapular) notch?

  1. Axillary nerve
  2. Suprascapular nerve
  3. Dorsal scapular nerve
  4. Upper subscapular nerve
Reveal answer

B — Suprascapular nerve (The suprascapular nerve enters the infraspinous fossa through the spinoglenoid notch to innervate the infraspinatus; compression here selectively weakens external rotation without affecting supraspinatus).

Brachial Plexus & Regional Nerves

11. The lateral cord of the brachial plexus is formed by the anterior divisions of which trunks?

  1. Upper and middle trunks
  2. Middle and lower trunks
  3. Upper, middle, and lower trunks
  4. Upper trunk only
Reveal answer

A — Upper and middle trunks (The lateral cord is formed by the anterior divisions of the upper and middle trunks, containing fibers from C5, C6, and C7).

12. Which nerve arises directly from the roots (ventral rami) of the brachial plexus rather than from trunks or cords?

  1. Suprascapular nerve
  2. Long thoracic nerve
  3. Lateral pectoral nerve
  4. Thoracodorsal nerve
Reveal answer

B — Long thoracic nerve (Arises directly from the ventral rami of C5, C6, and C7).

13. In Erb-Duchenne palsy (upper trunk brachial plexus injury, C5–C6), the characteristically seen "waiter’s tip" deformity results in which limb position?

  1. Abducted, externally rotated, elbow flexed
  2. Adducted, internally rotated, elbow extended, forearm pronated
  3. Flexed at elbow, wrist extended, forearm supinated
  4. Complete clawing of all digits with wrist drop
Reveal answer

B — Adducted, internally rotated, elbow extended, forearm pronated (Loss of abductors [deltoid, supraspinatus], lateral rotators [infraspinatus, teres minor], and elbow flexors/supinators [biceps, brachialis]).

14. A lower trunk brachial plexus injury (Klumpke’s palsy, C8–T1) is associated with Horner’s syndrome because of disruption of:

  1. Cervical sympathetic trunk fibers traveling with the T1 ventral ramus
  2. Phrenic nerve contributions to the sympathetic chain
  3. Somatic motor branches to the dilator pupillae
  4. The recurrent laryngeal nerve
Reveal answer

A — Cervical sympathetic trunk fibers traveling with the T1 ventral ramus (T1 carries preganglionic sympathetic fibers heading to the superior cervical ganglion; disruption causes ptosis, miosis, and anhidrosis).

15. The musculocutaneous nerve terminates in the forearm as which sensory nerve?

  1. Posterior cutaneous nerve of the forearm
  2. Lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm
  3. Medial cutaneous nerve of the forearm
  4. Superficial branch of radial nerve
Reveal answer

B — Lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm (After emerging lateral to the biceps tendon, the musculocutaneous nerve continues into the forearm as the lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm).

Arm & Cubital Fossa

16. Which muscle receives a dual innervation from both the musculocutaneous nerve and the radial nerve?

  1. Biceps brachii
  2. Coracobrachialis
  3. Brachialis
  4. Brachioradialis
Reveal answer

C — Brachialis (Brachialis is primarily innervated by the musculocutaneous nerve, with its inferolateral portion receiving proprioceptive/motor fibers from the radial nerve).

17. A fracture of the midshaft of the humerus at the radial (spiral) groove most commonly injures the radial nerve along with which blood vessel?

  1. Anterior circumflex humeral artery
  2. Superior ulnar collateral artery
  3. Profunda brachii artery
  4. Common interosseous artery
Reveal answer

C — Profunda brachii artery (Both travel together in the radial [spiral] groove along the posterior surface of the humeral shaft).

18. Which of the following forms the floor of the cubital fossa?

  1. Biceps tendon and pronator teres
  2. Brachialis and supinator
  3. Pronator teres and brachioradialis
  4. Bicipital aponeurosis and deep fascia
Reveal answer

B — Brachialis and supinator (Brachialis forms the medial part of the floor; supinator forms the lateral part).

19. In the cubital fossa, the brachial artery lies in which anatomical relationship to the median nerve?

  1. Medial to the median nerve
  2. Lateral to the median nerve
  3. Superficial to the median nerve
  4. Posterior to the bicipital aponeurosis and lateral to the biceps tendon
Reveal answer

B — Lateral to the median nerve (From medial to lateral, the deep contents of the cubital fossa are: Median nerve, Brachial artery, Biceps tendon [MN-BA-Bi]).

20. Which superficial vein ascends along the lateral aspect of the arm and pierces the clavipectoral fascia at the deltopectoral triangle?

  1. Basilic vein
  2. Cephalic vein
  3. Median cubital vein
  4. Axillary vein
Reveal answer

B — Cephalic vein (Ascends on the anterolateral aspect of the upper limb and drains into the axillary vein within the deltopectoral triangle).

Forearm: Anterior Compartment & Flexor Mechanism

21. Which flexor muscle of the forearm is supplied by the ulnar nerve?

  1. Pronator teres
  2. Flexor carpi radialis
  3. Flexor carpi ulnaris
  4. Palmaris longus
Reveal answer

C — Flexor carpi ulnaris (Innervated by the ulnar nerve; all other superficial anterior forearm flexors are supplied by the median nerve).

22. The anterior interosseous nerve, a branch of the median nerve, supplies which of the following muscles?

  1. Flexor digitorum superficialis
  2. Pronator quadratus
  3. Brachioradialis
  4. Abductor pollicis brevis
Reveal answer

B — Pronator quadratus (The anterior interosseous nerve supplies the deep flexors: flexor pollicis longus, lateral half of flexor digitorum profundus, and pronator quadratus).

23. The tendon of which muscle passes through a separate lateral compartment (split tunnel) within the flexor retinaculum?

  1. Flexor carpi radialis
  2. Flexor carpi ulnaris
  3. Flexor pollicis longus
  4. Palmaris longus
Reveal answer

A — Flexor carpi radialis (FCR passes through its own compartment within the lateral attachment of the flexor retinaculum, groove on the trapezium).

24. In the distal forearm, the radial artery lies directly between which two muscle tendons, making it an ideal site for palpating the pulse?

  1. Flexor carpi radialis and pronator teres
  2. Brachioradialis and flexor carpi radialis
  3. Palmaris longus and flexor carpi ulnaris
  4. Abductor pollicis longus and extensor pollicis brevis
Reveal answer

B — Brachioradialis and flexor carpi radialis (The radial pulse is palpated on the distal radius between the tendons of brachioradialis laterally and FCR medially).

25. The common interosseous artery is a direct branch of which vessel?

  1. Radial artery
  2. Ulnar artery
  3. Brachial artery
  4. Deep palmar arch
Reveal answer

B — Ulnar artery (The common interosseous artery arises from the ulnar artery shortly after its origin in the proximal forearm).

Forearm: Posterior Compartment & Dorsum of Hand

26. Which muscle initiates supinating action when the elbow is extended and the movement is slow and unresisted?

  1. Biceps brachii
  2. Pronator teres
  3. Supinator
  4. Anconeus
Reveal answer

C — Supinator (The supinator is the prime mover during slow, unresisted supination with the elbow extended; biceps brachii acts during rapid or resisted supination with the elbow flexed).

27. The posterior interosseous nerve enters the posterior compartment of the forearm by passing between the two heads of which muscle?

  1. Pronator teres
  2. Supinator (arcade of Fröhse)
  3. Anconeus
  4. Extensor carpi radialis brevis
Reveal answer

B — Supinator (arcade of Fröhse) (The deep branch of the radial nerve pierces supinator and emerges on its posterior surface as the posterior interosseous nerve).

28. The medial boundary of the anatomical snuffbox is formed by the tendon of:

  1. Extensor pollicis longus
  2. Extensor pollicis brevis
  3. Abductor pollicis longus
  4. Extensor indicis
Reveal answer

A — Extensor pollicis longus (Medial boundary: EPL tendon; Lateral boundary: EPB and APL tendons).

29. Which bone forms the floor of the anatomical snuffbox and is most susceptible to fracture after a fall on an outstretched hand?

  1. Pisiform
  2. Hamate
  3. Scaphoid
  4. Triquetrum
Reveal answer

C — Scaphoid (The scaphoid and trapezium form the floor; the scaphoid is the most commonly fractured carpal bone).

30. Lister’s dorsal tubercle on the distal end of the radius acts as a pulley for which muscle tendon?

  1. Extensor carpi radialis longus
  2. Extensor digitorum
  3. Extensor pollicis longus
  4. Extensor digiti minimi
Reveal answer

C — Extensor pollicis longus (Lister’s tubercle redirects the tendon of EPL toward the thumb).

The Hand: Fascial Spaces, Intrinsic Muscles & Vessels

31. Which intrinsic hand muscle is innervated by the median nerve?

  1. Adductor pollicis
  2. Palmar interossei
  3. First lumbrical
  4. Abductor digiti minimi
Reveal answer

C — First lumbrical (The 1st and 2nd lumbricals, along with thenar muscles [OAF: Opponens pollicis, Abductor pollicis brevis, superficial head of Flexor pollicis brevis], are median nerve innervated).

32. The deep palmar arterial arch is primarily formed by the terminal continuation of the:

  1. Ulnar artery
  2. Radial artery
  3. Anterior interosseous artery
  4. Superficial palmar branch of radial artery
Reveal answer

B — Radial artery (The radial artery enters the palm between the heads of the first dorsal interosseous and completes the deep palmar arch with the deep branch of the ulnar artery).

33. Guyon’s canal (pisohamate canal) transmits which nerve and vessel into the hand?

  1. Median nerve and superficial palmar branch
  2. Radial nerve (superficial branch) and radial artery
  3. Ulnar nerve and ulnar artery
  4. Anterior interosseous nerve and artery
Reveal answer

C — Ulnar nerve and ulnar artery (Pass superficial to the flexor retinaculum through Guyon's canal).

34. How many tendons pass through the carpal tunnel deep to the flexor retinaculum?

  1. 8
  2. 9
  3. 10
  4. 11
Reveal answer

B — 9 (4 tendons of FDS, 4 tendons of FDP, and 1 tendon of FPL pass through the tunnel alongside the median nerve).

35. The dorsal interossei muscles of the hand are responsible for:

  1. Adduction of digits toward the axis of the 3rd digit
  2. Abduction of digits away from the axis of the 3rd digit
  3. Flexion of distal interphalangeal joints
  4. Extension of metacarpophalangeal joints
Reveal answer

B — Abduction of digits away from the axis of the 3rd digit (Mnemonic: DAB = Dorsal Interossei ABduct; PAD = Palmar Interossei ADduct).

36. The lumbrical muscles uniquely act to produce which combined joint movement?

  1. Extension of MCP joints and flexion of IP joints
  2. Flexion of MCP joints and extension of IP joints
  3. Flexion of both MCP and IP joints
  4. Abduction and adduction of digits
Reveal answer

B — Flexion of MCP joints and extension of IP joints (Via attachments to the radial sides of the extensor expansions).

37. The midpalmar space communicates distally with the web spaces of the fingers primarily via the:

  1. Carpal tunnel
  2. Lumbrical canals
  3. Space of Parona
  4. Thenar space
Reveal answer

B — Lumbrical canals (Lumbrical canals act as anatomical passages carrying pus from the midpalmar space to the web spaces of digits 2 to 4).

38. The ulnar bursa commonly acts as the synovial sheath enclosing the tendons of:

  1. Flexor pollicis longus
  2. Flexor carpi radialis
  3. Flexor digitorum superficialis and profundus
  4. Extensor digitorum
Reveal answer

C — Flexor digitorum superficialis and profundus (The ulnar bursa is the common synovial sheath for the 8 tendons of FDS and FDP).

Joints & Biomechanics

39. The first carpometacarpal joint (trapeziometacarpal joint of the thumb) is anatomically classified as a:

  1. Plane synovial joint
  2. Hinge joint
  3. Saddle (sellar) joint
  4. Ball-and-socket (spheroidal) joint
Reveal answer

C — Saddle (sellar) joint (Provides reciprocal concavo-convex surfaces allowing flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, and axial rotation [opposition]).

40. The carrying angle of the elbow joint is normally formed due to the:

  1. Superior projection of the radial head
  2. Medial flange of the trochlea projecting lower than the lateral flange
  3. Curvature of the olecranon process
  4. Angulation of the bicipital tuberosity
Reveal answer

B — Medial flange of the trochlea projecting lower than the lateral flange (This anatomical asymmetry angles the extended ulna outward 10°–15° relative to the humerus).

41. During forearm pronation and supination, which ligament maintains the radial head against the radial notch of the ulna?

  1. Quadrate ligament
  2. Annular ligament
  3. Oblique cord
  4. Interosseous membrane
Reveal answer

B — Annular ligament (Encircles the head of the radius and holds it in the radial notch of the ulna).

42. The wrist (radiocarpal) joint involves articulation between the distal radius and which carpal bones?

  1. Scaphoid, lunate, and triquetrum
  2. Scaphoid, trapezium, and trapezoid
  3. Lunate, triquetrum, and pisiform
  4. Scaphoid, capitate, and hamate
Reveal answer

A — Scaphoid, lunate, and triquetrum (The articular disc excludes the ulna; the distal radial surface articulates with scaphoid and lunate, and triquetrum in full adduction).

43. Which ligament provides the principal dynamic and static support to prevent downward displacement of the clavicle and shoulder girdle?

  1. Acromioclavicular ligament
  2. Coracoclavicular ligament (trapezoid and conoid parts)
  3. Costoclavicular ligament
  4. Sternoclavicular ligament
Reveal answer

B — Coracoclavicular ligament (trapezoid and conoid parts) (Provides the strongest suspension anchoring the clavicle to the coracoid process).

Clinical & Applied Anatomy

44. A laceration of the palmar aspect of the wrist cutting the median nerve just proximal to the flexor retinaculum preserves sensation over which area?

  1. Palmar surface of the index finger
  2. Palmar surface of the thumb
  3. Proximal central palm over the thenar eminence
  4. Palmar surface of the middle finger
Reveal answer

C — Proximal central palm over the thenar eminence (The palmar cutaneous branch of the median nerve branches proximal to the flexor retinaculum and passes superficial to it, sparing sensation here in carpal tunnel syndrome).

45. Froment’s paper sign tests for paralysis of the adductor pollicis, indicating injury to which nerve?

  1. Median nerve
  2. Ulnar nerve
  3. Radial nerve
  4. Anterior interosseous nerve
Reveal answer

B — Ulnar nerve (Paralysis of adductor pollicis leads to compensatory hyperflexion of the thumb interphalangeal joint via FPL [median nerve] to hold paper).

46. A patient who sustains an anterior dislocation of the shoulder is examined for nerve injury. Which physical finding confirms axillary nerve involvement?

  1. Inability to flex the elbow
  2. Sensory loss over the lateral aspect of the shoulder (regimental badge area)
  3. Winging of the scapula
  4. Numbness on the dorsum of the first web space
Reveal answer

B — Sensory loss over the lateral aspect of the shoulder (regimental badge area) (The superior lateral cutaneous nerve of the arm, a branch of the axillary nerve, supplies this area).

47. Winging of the scapula occurs after damage to the long thoracic nerve. This nerve courses along the external surface of which muscle?

  1. Latissimus dorsi
  2. Pectoralis minor
  3. Subscapularis
  4. Serratus anterior
Reveal answer

D — Serratus anterior (The long thoracic nerve descends on the external surface of serratus anterior in the medial wall of the axilla).

48. A child is brought to the clinic with subluxation of the radial head ("nursemaid’s elbow") caused by sudden upward traction on a pronated forearm. The displaced structure is the:

  1. Radial collateral ligament
  2. Annular ligament
  3. Tendon of biceps brachii
  4. Ulnar collateral ligament
Reveal answer

B — Annular ligament (The radial head slips out from under the lax annular ligament, which becomes pinched between the radial head and capitulum).

49. In supracondylar fracture of the humerus, the anteriorly displaced proximal bony spike puts which artery and nerve at greatest risk of entrapment?

  1. Profunda brachii artery and radial nerve
  2. Posterior circumflex humeral artery and axillary nerve
  3. Brachial artery and median nerve
  4. Ulnar artery and ulnar nerve
Reveal answer

C — Brachial artery and median nerve (The anteriorly displaced proximal fragment can kink or pierce the brachial artery and impinge on the median nerve in the cubital fossa).

50. The anatomical basis of the "ulnar paradox" is that a lesion of the ulnar nerve at the elbow produces less pronounced clawing of the 4th and 5th digits than a lesion at the wrist because of concurrent paralysis of:

  1. Flexor carpi ulnaris
  2. Medial half of flexor digitorum profundus
  3. Adductor pollicis
  4. Flexor digiti minimi
Reveal answer

B — Medial half of flexor digitorum profundus (In high ulnar lesions, loss of the FDP tendon pull prevents flexion of the distal interphalangeal joints of digits 4 and 5, producing a less marked claw posture than in low wrist lesions).
